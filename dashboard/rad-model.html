Why biased irradiation collapses the SiN device and not the HPD one

A pre/post-irradiation model for the Y2 high-LET set (HPD #11 with HfO₂ passivation vs SiN #35, both irradiated at Vg −10 V, Vd 28 V). The stress bias places damage where the field is strongest; the SiN passivation focuses that field into one spot just past the gate, and that spot is what collapses the drain current.

The mechanism in one picture

Not to scale vertically. Under stress the field peaks at the gate's drain edge in both devices; there is no electron population there and the model leaves it undamaged (a fitting choice, see Limits). The second peak, under the T-gate overhang near y = 0.5 µm, is twice as high with SiN. Damage scales as field⁴, so that spot gets about ten times more traps in SiN.

How a run works

Every device is computed in the same order. Pre-irradiation devices stop after step 1; non-biased devices skip the stress-placed damage.

1 · calibrate

Pre-irradiation deck

Polarization charge 1e13 cm⁻², Schottky barrier 2.94 eV, mobility vsat 1.75e7 cm/s, µmax 2100. Vth within 0.03 V, Id–Vd at Vg +1 within ~4%.

2 · stress

Off-state bias

Gate to −10 V, then the drain in 1 V steps. The solve converges up to Vd 17 V (target 28 V), and the lateral field there is stored.

3 · damage

Traps from the stress field

Acceptor traps at 0.30 eV in the top 50 nm, density 7e18 · (E/5e5 V/cm)⁴ beyond y = 0.30 µm. All devices: fixed charge 5e17 cm⁻³ in the AlGaN under the gate and −4e11 cm⁻² at the surface.

4 · measure

Hot-electron capture

Id–Vg at Vd 0.1 V and Id–Vd at Vg +1 V. Hot electrons fill the traps once they clear a 0.5 eV barrier, so the current drops only above a threshold Vd.

Why the passivation matters

The two devices share the same gate, field plate and mesh; only the passivation's permittivity differs (εr 35 for HfO₂, 6.3 for SiN).

  • HighK couples the T-gate head and field plate strongly to the channel and spreads the drain voltage over several µm toward the drain.
  • SiN couples weakly, so the potential drops in a short stretch under the T-gate overhang: a second field peak at y ≈ 0.5 µm, about 5.5e5 V/cm against 2.9e5.
  • With a fourth-power damage law that factor of two becomes roughly 10× more traps, enough to trap the channel open only in SiN.

Before irradiation the two decks give the same current to within 1%, as the measured pre-irradiation curves do.

What the model reproduces

In the SiN device the current follows the pre-irradiation curve with a low-Vd loss until hot electrons reach the damage, then drops in a step near 4.5 V. A second, hand-placed strike site further toward the drain adds a second step near 5.8 V; after it the current creeps back up, as on the curve tracer.

The HighK device keeps its shape, a few percent below pre-irradiation.

What was tried and dropped

Did not separate the decks

  • Plain trap model at Vg +1: same onset on both decks 47062225
  • Damage ∝ field or field², and peak-field placement: equal in both 47327524 · 47356193
  • Fixed charge in the SiN layer from the dielectric field 47458257
  • Uniform damage at a given position: collapses HighK too 47458256 · 47606666
  • Electron-weighted damage (n·E, n·E⁴): contact damage dominates, or no effect 47628955 · 47725314

Changed the shape the wrong way

  • Damage at the gate edge: early collapse in both decks 47458256
  • Access-region n·E damage: more loss in HighK than SiN 47662463 · 47752290
  • Slower capture (τ 1e-12 s): full SiN collapse from 1 V 47773690
  • Under-gate charge through the channel: shifts Vth but costs ~7% gm; fixed by keeping it in the AlGaN 47852523

Limits

Numerical

  • The stress solve stops at Vd 17 V (gate–drain 27 V); the electron quasi-Fermi level diverges in the depleted region under the gate. Solving without holes and heavier damping do not help 47861047 · 47875456. The damage scale absorbs the weaker field.
  • A stronger second strike site deepens the SiN drop but the Id–Vd sweep cannot get through the step 47908735 · 47943462.

Fitting choices

  • No damage closer than 0.30 µm to the gate centre.
  • The second strike site is placed by hand in the SiN device; the same site in HighK would collapse it too, so it stands for where that ion happened to hit.
  • Gate–drain leakage is a lumped resistor per device.
